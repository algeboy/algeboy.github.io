Logical AND by Syntax And Semantics

The English word “AND” is a bit too generic to make a reliable logical operator. For instance, we might write

  • 12 is positive and even
  • Study and you will get good grades.

In the first sentence 12 is given two independent properties happen to coexist. Meanwhile the second sentence seems to indicate that the first property (studying) is responsible for the second (good grades). If you need further confirmation that something is different about these two uses consider swapping the order.

  • 12 is even and positive
  • You will get good grades and study.

Arguably the meaning of the first sentence is unchanged but the second really has changed in meaning.

Clarity through Syntax

In order to be extra clear about our logic we want to set aside the words in natural language an use instead symbols or words in special fonts so as to treat the rules with some rigor that cannot miscommunicate out meaning. To so this we start to make a system that resembles arithmetic with is various additions, subtractions and other operators. When we calculate with any symbols we get what is known as a “Calculus”

So far we have seen ()-calculus and now we will be looking at two further Calculi, Propositional and Sequent. This is as good a place as any to pause and mention the list of “calculi” that exist in this course.

  • Integral Calculus – the calculation of area and volume.
  • Differential Calculus – the calculation of rates of change.
  • Propositional Calculus – the way to compute with of propositions.
  • Lambda Calculus – the method of creating functions (funny name owing to a funny notation).
  • Sequent Calculus – the method to write down logic.

As a cultural warning, when some says they are studying “Calculus” chances are they mean one of the first two. Our main objective is to start making logical operators and we will focus on “AND”. In assignments you will explore “OR” and “NOT”

Sequents

Sequents means “parts of as a sequence” and the entire idea is to separate the premise from a conclusion. The devices to separate can just be a line, a long space, a word like “entails” but the most common one today is the turnstyle ⊢also said “vdash” since that is how you look it up as a font.

Sequents calculus is the use of sequence to calculate truth in a specified logic. If our logic only has “and” we can only calculate how make an and (introduction) and how to apply an and (elimination). All this must be written in a language.

  • (L)anguage : the alphabat, symbols, and grammar to be used in the sequents. If your sequents do not parse in the grammar then they don’t count.
  • (I)ntroduction : what premise or premises do you need to get ot use your logical operator?
  • (E)limination : what premises along with context do you need to use a logical operator?

The LIE components of a logical operator are a good way to remember the steps.

Semantics

There is another way we can describe logical operators that get closer to our appreciation of what our data means. For example, 12 is even and positive is true because each of “12 is even” and “12 is positive” are true. If this is all we care about we can ignore the precise form in which we get the facts and focus purely on the values. This is known as “Semantic” reasoning.

When it comes the semantics of AND we insist on one rule:

Only True AND True should be True

However this still leaves us lots of room to explore meaning.

The point is, if we look at AND as is values instead of its syntax we have the freedom to engineer how we actually apply AND. That can be a powerful feature.

Many valued logical AND

Finally, much of life is not so certain and while we may describe some things as true () we might struggle to conclude that all other things are false (). For that many logics have been created that use further values. Sometimes it is a U for “Unknown”. Some times a “M” for mixed” (maybe a concept that has elements of truth and falsity), or “F(x)” for a “fuzzy truth (0x)” to measure quantities that vary continuously.

I like to use the symbol (+) because it seems typographically between () and () and reminds me we could slide the bar up or down to introduce many subdivisions of “truth”. Obeying the rule of and: ONLY TRUE AND TRUE IS TRUE, we are still permitted to assign the remains values in different ways. For example, perhaps (+) should be (+), meaning, if you have something true and something partially true, then the whole thing is partially true. Of course context gets to decide what will count as the new meaning of AND. We close by detailing one of the ANDs as defined by the logician Kleene.

In words: truth and truth is true; truth and partial truth is partially true but any value and a falsity is false. There are clearly many ways that this captures the mode of a logic for AND even when we cannot be certain that our data is good enough to make things exactly true or false. And to make this semantics into arithmetic, suppose we used 1 for (), 0 for (+), and -1 of (). Then (PQ) becomes merely ((P,Q)). For example ((1,0)=0) which is what it means to have True and Partially true being only Partially true.

As you develop your logic you may have many other ways to approach operators like this one. But resist the urge to call it an AND unless it obey the main rule: Only True and True is True.
