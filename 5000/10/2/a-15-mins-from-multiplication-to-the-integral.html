10.2.a (15 mins) From multiplication to the Integral

Many measurements you will encounter are accumulations of smaller measurements. If we learn to compute those measurements the rest is data collection and physical scanners.

Simple functions

Suppose we know how to measure disjoint subsets (A_1,, A_n). Then we can measure them together because the whole is the sum of its parts:

((A_1A_n) = (A_1)++(A_n))

Products of measures: the integral

To explore the product of two measures let’s call them collectively as “Area”. In a rectangle this is length of the best times the length of the height. Yet concepts like length and area do not mean anything to topics like Market Capitalization, Torque or many other product related measurements. Yet it helps us often to recycle the language of shapes so we are guided towards tools that will help us work with those topics. So throughout we put quotation marks around these words that are metaphorical abstractions, e.g. “Area” and “Length”.

An “area” from the stock market

The “Cumulative Stock Price” of a company is the sum of changing stock prices in some time window. This may not sound like an area, but we attach that vocabulary by plotting a diagram of the stock. Building the Integral

A “rectangle’s area” (x) and (y)

To kick off the measurement let us put some names and symbols to the parameters.

  • (x) will be mean the (signed or unsigned) measurement on the x-axis of our “rectangle”. This could be a distance measure, a probability measure or some unknown measure, signed or unsigned. However, the images we plot to explain this will all be visualizations of the distance measure.
  • (y) will be mean the (signed or unsigned) measurement on the y-axis of our “rectangle”.
  • (xy) the measure of the “rectangle” with sides (y) and (x).

Keep in mind these are metaphorical concepts. The pictures are therefore not necessary, but they are helpful to many.

Approximating complicated “Areas” with “Rectangle areas”

It would be difficult to guess the precise area in something as complicated as the Nvidia stock prices. However we know that whatever a graphical “area” is meant to be it should 2-dimensional and have a 1-dimensional boundary. So if we are to produce that concept in other applications we need to declare upfront what is our boundary. We can do this with a combination of boundaries, (think bottom, sides, top). For example, in our Cumulative Stock Price

  • the bottom () was the x-axis (because stock prices cannot go negative).
  • The sides were the window time, say 2021 on the left and 2026 on the right.
  • The top is the interesting bit, it is the daily stock prices, some crazy graph (f).

Now for an approximation we can use the Stock prices at several places to set up how high our rectangle goes. And we can widen or shrink our rectangles by taking points further or closer apart. In the end we have a mix of over and under estimates of the area.

Let us label each rectangle by the pair of intervals (X=[x_j,x_{j+1}]) and (Y=[y_j,y_{j+1}]) that define each rectangles dimensions (X) and (Y) respectively. All the rectangles collectively are (). So if (A) is the target area, our approximation by rectangles is trying to approximate () as a disjoint union.

Thus we can use the fact that for measures, the whole is the sum of its parts((AB)=(A)+(B)). We have finite number of parts so we distribute the measurement as sum of each rectangle measurement.

The integral (_{} f d) as the best approximation. Lebesgue’s Method.

Now to make the best estimate suppose insist that our approximations are all from below the graph, written

(f) the rectangles in (mathcal{P}) are entirely below the graph of (f)

Now define the integral as the least upper bound / supremum of all approximations.

This integral was created by Lebesgue (le-bay-guh) and it is the integral that you will need in data science, probability, mathematics and science.

Technical Glitch. While this description is the right concept, sometimes no single () reaches the exact area. So there is no max. Instead there might be gradually improving max. So we should take the limit of this growing approximation.

Mathematicians have given the limit of maximum values a name. We call it a supremum instead of max.

the limit of all subsets that have a maximum.

So the actual statement should use supremum not max.

You can probably get by confusing maximum with supremum but at least you have been warned that eventually you could need to know the difference.

Riemann’s alternative for continuous data (Optional)

Sometimes it is nice to have more than one way to think or compute and the integral is not exception. One strategy that has had enormous success for geometry and physics problem has concerned the situations where the domain of integration is uniform (compact is the mathematician’s term). of our area are all connected by continuous lines. (Unlike our weighted stock model). more than it is not necessary to have more than one way to compute integrals, the integral we have defined works for all measurements, there is a popular alternative designed specifically for geometry. If you have seen calculus then chances are the this is the integral you were taught.

We should think of the change in the x-axis as striding between (a) and (b), so it is sequence (a=x_0<x_1<< x_n=b).

Each change in x is the measure of that stride:

(x_j = x_{j+1}-x_j)

Since we measure the height from the x-axis, a height of 0, and we stop when we reach the curve (f), the change in y is

(y_j=f(x_j)-0)

Take as a sum we have the formula at any one approximation. Taking all the approximations together we can ask for the limit (if it exists) and give that a similar name. The giant S () to invoke the growing size of the sums over increasing numbers of boxes, and the lower case d (dx) to invoke the shrinking measure of each stride in the x axis.

This integral is credited to Riemann though it appeared in various forms in earlier work. While some people find this model easier to learn, it does not work with many of the important applications of measurement, including probability and data science applications. So it is best to invest in our first integral.
