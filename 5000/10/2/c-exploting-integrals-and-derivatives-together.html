10.2.c. Exploting Integrals & Derivatives Together

The power of measurement is strongest when we can go forward with integrals and backwards with derivatives.

y=(y*x)/x and therefore Calculus

A bit of history and context

Historically derivatives were defined as the slope of tangents to a curve or instantaneous rates of change. These concepts are not immediately logical—how do you define a unique tangent to a graph without already having the slope, i.e. it makes for a circular definition. An instant in time is also not a precise concept. But its ability to solve questions made Newton (1660’s) a hero of science. Between the 1800-1900’s Cauchy, Weirestrass, and Riemann each contributed ideas to make the concept precise. Still issued persisted not least of which was the difficulty in stacking integrals measurements. While geometry suggested we can remeasure as many times as we like, the formulas of the Cauchy-Riemann method had gaps and oddities that did not match the graphical intuition.

To solve these problem Lebesgue’s integral (1900) threw the subject on its head. Instead of beginning with the concept of smaller and smaller approximations, Lebesgue classified what a measurement under the basic concept that: the whole is the sum of its parts. Once created integrals stand out as accumulation of measurements under any grouping into parts – be those small approximations or large chunks. The concept now being sound later authors such as Radon-Nikodym (1915) explored how to convert the calculus of the previous generation into this formalism. This new integral has proved compelling flexible and useful so it has dominated the sciences. For data sciences and probability it is the main tool. Icon Progress Bar (browser only)
